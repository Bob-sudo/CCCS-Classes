your answer should be of the form $O(n^k)$ for $k=0, 1, 2, 3, ...$.
Of course $O(n^0)$ is $O(1)$.

\textsc{Note.}
For all $n$, it's possible that the descending array will take more
wallclock time to sort than an ascending array, but
asymptotically speaking, their runtimes can be the same, such as $O(n^2)$.
In general, it's possible that for an algorithm for arrays,
a particular pattern will require more wallclock time to process
than another pattern, but their asymptotic runtimes (in $n$, the size
of the array) might very well be the same.
